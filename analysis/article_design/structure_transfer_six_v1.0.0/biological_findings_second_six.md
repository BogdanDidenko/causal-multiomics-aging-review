# What a Disjoint Six-Report Subset Adds

Source-checked working synthesis, 2026-09-21.

| Study | Aging question and system | Main result | What the omics data contributed | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S1. Urolithin A, mitophagy and longevity** [Roussos et al.](https://doi.org/10.1080/15548627.2025.2561073) | Whether urolithin A (UA) preserves function and lifespan through calcium-dependent organelle communication in *C. elegans*, with mechanistic checks in human cells. | Calcium chelation reduced UA-associated lifespan benefit and abolished its locomotor benefit. Loss of ITR-1, MCU-1 or DRP-1 disrupted UA-induced mitophagy, healthspan or longevity. UA also increased calcium-dependent mitophagy in normal human fibroblasts and endothelial cells. | Worm transcriptomics and proteomics nominated coordinated calcium, lysosomal, mitochondrial and ER responses. Those candidates guided genetic and pharmacological perturbations across the proposed pathway. | Strong mechanistic evidence exists within worms and cultured cells. It does not establish lifespan extension, rejuvenation or clinical efficacy in naturally aging humans. The many perturbations are nested tests of one UA-centered mechanism, not independent studies. |
| **S2. Nitrogen and premature leaf senescence** [Huo et al.](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14152388) | Whether an assigned nitrogen treatment delays premature senescence in potted red raspberry plants. | Compared with no nitrogen, N150 plants showed less lower-leaf yellowing, improved growth and pigment measures, and lower oxidative-damage markers. The experiment identified 4,350 differentially expressed genes and 135 differential metabolites. | Integrated transcriptomics and metabolomics localized the response to phenylpropanoid and flavonoid pathways and highlighted chlorogenic acid and naringenin chalcone. | Nitrogen assignment supports a treatment effect on the measured plant phenotype. The proposed causal roles of individual metabolites were not directly perturbed and remain mechanistic hypotheses. This is plant premature senescence, not mammalian biological aging. |
| **S3. Temperature and pear-fruit senescence** [Gu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41438-020-00420-y) | How assigned high- and low-temperature storage changes postharvest pear senescence. | Fruit reached senescence after 15 days at high temperature, 19 days at room temperature and 105 days at low temperature. Pbr027651.1 overexpression delayed senescence, its silencing accelerated senescence, and Novel_188 overexpression accelerated senescence while repressing Pbr027651.1. | Metabolomics, transcriptomics and microRNA profiling nominated temperature-specific networks and the Novel_188–Pbr027651.1 link, which was then tested using luciferase and transient-transformation experiments. | The targeted link has perturbational support, while most of the wider network remains based on differential abundance, target prediction and correlation. The endpoint is postharvest fruit senescence and should remain a distinct boundary stratum. |
| **S4. Senescence signature in cervical cancer** [Chen et al.](https://doi.org/10.21037/tcr-2026-1-0264) | Whether a senescence-related expression signature predicts survival, metastasis, immune state and drug sensitivity in cervical cancer. | The report evaluates prognostic ROC curves, survival strata, metastasis prediction, immune associations and predicted drug sensitivity. Terra and Opus independently found no qualifying causal analysis. | Expression-derived features support risk prediction and biological stratification. They do not provide causal leverage over senescence, treatment or aging outcomes. | Prediction, differential analysis and correlation cannot support a causal-evidence synthesis. This report requires eligibility re-adjudication under EC5 and must not be counted as causal evidence pending that decision. |
| **S5. Mitochondrial DNA and epigenetic aging** [Wang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.70279) | Whether mitochondrial DNA copy number and epigenetic-age acceleration are directionally related and whether DunedinPACE mediates links with obesity and type 2 diabetes. | In older FHS participants, DunedinPACE mediated 11.1% and 10.8% of the mtDNA-copy-number associations with obesity and diabetes. Meta-analytic mediation evidence was suggestive rather than significant, and JHS effects were near zero. Forward MR evidence was method-specific and weak; reverse MR was null. | Mitochondrial metrics and DNA-methylation clocks were integrated across cohorts, while nuclear GWAS instruments supplied the MR leverage. | Cross-sectional mediation cannot establish temporal direction. The forward MR signal was unsupported by the primary IVW estimate, and replication was inconsistent. The evidence supports a qualified directional hypothesis rather than a robust intervention claim. |
| **S6. Physical activity mediation and AD-like atrophy** [Xu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13040-025-00432-1) | Whether physical-activity patterns mediate associations between genetic variants and an imaging-derived AD-like brain-atrophy index. | BAMA retained 23 signals involving three SNPs, but none of those signals reached significance in the univariate mediation analysis. The report lacked an external replication dataset. | Genomics supplies the molecular layer; accelerometry supplies candidate mediators and MRI supplies the outcome. The mediation model imposes a directed structure across these data types. | Accelerometry and imaging are not molecular omics under the review protocol, so multi-omics eligibility requires re-adjudication. PIP greater than zero was explicitly exploratory, model assumptions were strong, and the result is hypothesis-level rather than an identified intervention effect. |

## Structural comparison with the first subset

The five reader-facing Results questions remain supported:

1. **Evidence base:** the second subset adds plant, fruit, cancer, molecular-clock and AD-proxy systems.
2. **Causal leverage:** assigned environmental or compound interventions, targeted perturbations, MR and formal mediation remain separable.
3. **Multi-omics contribution:** omics nominate targets, measure intervention response and connect molecular layers; the subset also exposes a multimodal-versus-molecular-omics boundary.
4. **Aging phenomenon:** lifespan and function coexist with cellular, plant, postharvest, epigenetic-clock and disease-proxy outcomes.
5. **Strength and transportability:** model-specific effects, method-sensitive estimates, null validation, absent replication and nonhuman systems all require explicit limits.

The top-level architecture therefore transfers across both subsets. The first
subset's three biological themes are insufficient as fixed Results headings.
They should remain provisional subthemes populated from the complete corpus.

This is a structural transfer result from twelve purposively diverse reports,
not a prevalence estimate or a statistical validation of the article outline.
