# Full-text comparator inventory

## AMD systematic review

**Citation:** Castro-Fernandez et al. *A systematic review on age-related
macular degeneration: New insights from multi-omics studies*. DOI:
`10.1016/j.survophthal.2026.02.001`.

### Structure

- Introduction: omics, AMD, limitations of single-omics reviews, and the need
  for cross-layer synthesis.
- Results 2.1: study characteristics.
- Results 2.2: OHAT quality assessment.
- Results 2.3: synthesis split into non-clinical and clinical evidence.
- Non-clinical subsections: risk factors, early processes, advanced processes,
  and treatment.
- Clinical subsections: local and systemic processes.
- Discussion: methodological trends, causal claims, spatial/single-cell
  approaches, convergent mechanisms, gaps, and translation.
- Conclusion and Methods appear after the Discussion.

### Displays

- Figure 1: PRISMA 2020 flow.
- Table 1: OHAT risk-of-bias matrix with eleven question domains.
- Table 2: 33-report evidence table spanning about five PDF pages. Columns:
  `Ref | Year | Research | Data source | Samples | Cohort characteristics |
  Omics approaches | Assay type | Main findings`.
- Figure 2: biological convergence across complement/inflammation,
  angiogenesis, mitochondrial dysfunction, and lipid dyshomeostasis.

### Reusable elements

- Clear separation of selection, characteristics, appraisal, and synthesis.
- Complete standardized evidence table.
- Second-pass biological interpretation after study mapping.
- Explicit clinical versus non-clinical translation boundary.

### Elements not to copy

- A single report-level OHAT matrix for heterogeneous causal designs.
- Burying causal method, estimand, and identification assumptions inside assay
  or findings columns.
- Mixing association, colocalization, perturbation, and causal-effect evidence
  within mechanism-based subsections.
- Scaling the five-page 33-report table directly to 101 eligible reports.

## Kerr rare-disease scoping review

**Citation:** Kerr et al. *A scoping review and proposed workflow for
multi-omic rare disease research*. DOI: `10.1186/s13023-020-01376-x`.

### Structure

- Background: rare-disease burden, diagnostic delay, genomic programs,
  multi-omics potential, and integration challenges.
- Aims and objectives framed through population, concept, and context.
- Methods: published protocol, JBI guidance, PRISMA-ScR, duplicate charting,
  critical appraisal, and narrative synthesis.
- Results: selection, study designs, participants, omics combinations, disease
  distribution, and rigor.
- Five themes identified in Results become Discussion subsections: enabling
  technologies; standardization; diagnosis; pathogenic/prognostic markers; and
  treatment or drug repurposing.
- Discussion ends in a proposed multi-omics research workflow.

### Displays

- Figure 2: PRISMA flow.
- Table 1: aggregate study characteristics.
- Figure 3: publications by year.
- Table 2: fourteen combinations of omics analyses.
- Table 3 and Figure 4: participant phenotypes and disease distribution.
- Table 4: disease prevalence, study references, and objectives.
- Figure 5: proposed workflow from study design through integration,
  validation, interpretation, and reporting.

### Reusable elements

- Protocol-driven review governance and explicit PCC framing.
- Duplicate extraction and adjudication.
- Quantitative field map before thematic synthesis.
- Map-to-theme-to-workflow narrative.
- Exhaustive study data in supplementary material rather than oversized main
  tables.

### Elements not to copy

- Generic high/medium/low rigor scores across heterogeneous designs.
- Diagnostic/pathogenic/therapeutic themes as substitutes for causal evidence
  functions.
- Moving substantial outcome synthesis from Results into Discussion.
- Omics combinations as the primary inferential hierarchy.

## Combined lesson

Use Kerr's review governance and field-map logic, then use the AMD review's
second-pass biological synthesis. Insert a causal evidence layer missing from
both: directed discovery, identified effects, and independent validation of the
same link must remain distinct throughout extraction, appraisal, displays, and
interpretation.
