# Recommended article architecture

## Article type and organizing principle

Use a **systematic evidence map with claim-level causal appraisal**. The primary
hierarchy is evidence function and causal design family:

1. **Causal hypothesis/discovery:** a formal directed method produces a testable
   causal hypothesis without sufficient effect identification.
2. **Causal-effect evidence:** a design supports an assessable causal contrast
   in a specified biological system.
3. **Independent validation/triangulation:** independent data or an orthogonal
   design evaluates the same normalized causal link.

Levels 0-4 implement these distinctions in extraction and audit. They are not
Results headings, a universal evidence pyramid, or a replacement for
design-specific credibility assessment.

## Proposed manuscript structure

### Structured abstract, 275-300 words

Background; objective; sources and eligibility; evidence-function
classification; principal landscape and gaps; conclusion. Report discovery,
effect, and validation evidence separately.

### 1. Introduction, 750-850 words

1. **Biological aging as a cross-layer causal problem.** Define the relevant
   aging constructs and explain that molecular layers may act as exposures,
   mediators, outcomes, or intervention responses.
2. **Multi-omics integration is not causal identification.** Separate
   association, formal directed discovery, causal-effect identification, and
   independent validation.
3. **Review gap and objectives.** State the eligibility boundary and the goal of
   mapping what each design can support, not merely which omics were measured.

### 2. Methods, 1,500-1,700 words

1. Protocol, reporting framework, amendments, and frozen search dates.
2. Eligibility and units of analysis, including report-to-study linkage.
3. Information sources, database-native searches, deduplication, retrieval,
   screening, and adjudication.
4. AI-assisted screening and audit controls: five runs, schema and quotation
   checks, disagreement routing, and human decisions.
5. Claim-level extraction: aging construct, population/model, tissue, omics
   layers, integration architecture, causal role, design family, estimand,
   assumptions, diagnostics, effect result, and validation.
6. Evidence-function definitions and internal Levels 0-4 mapping.
7. Design-specific credibility assessment.
8. Synthesis, handling of dependencies, and visualization rules.

### 3. Results, 2,500-2,800 words

1. **Study selection and analytic corpus.** Show PRISMA, report linkage,
   versions, shared cohorts/data, and the transition from 101 full-text-eligible
   candidates to the final extracted synthesis set.
2. **Causal-design evidence landscape.** Describe evidence function and design
   family first; then aging constructs, populations/models, tissues, omics
   combinations, integration strategies, and data-source overlap.
3. **Formal causal hypothesis and discovery.** Report methods, orientation
   constraints, learned directions, robustness, and external checks. Do not call
   directed edges effect estimates.
4. **Causal-effect evidence by identification strategy.** Use subsections for
   genetic instruments; randomized, quasi-experimental, and direct perturbation
   designs; and temporal, mediation, and structural effect designs.
5. **Independent validation and triangulation.** Require a normalized
   exposure-to-outcome match. Distinguish external cohorts, cross-system or
   cross-species tests, orthogonal assays, and perturbation validation from
   same-data robustness checks.
6. **Aging mechanisms across evidence functions.** Reorganize by recurrent
   aging mechanism only after every link has an evidence-function label.
7. **Credibility, reporting, and transportability gaps.** Summarize assumptions,
   diagnostics, negative controls, sensitivity analyses, data overlap,
   replication, ancestry, sex, tissue, and species limitations.

### 4. Discussion, 1,650-1,850 words

1. Principal findings and the balance between discovery, effect, and validation.
2. What multi-omics adds to causal aging research.
3. What the evidence does and does not identify.
4. Validation, reproducibility, and transportability.
5. Design and reporting implications, expressed as a causal multi-omics
   workflow or checklist.
6. Review strengths and limitations, including AI-assisted screening.
7. Research agenda.

### 5. Conclusion, 150-180 words

State which conclusions are hypothesis-level, which have assessable effect
support, and which have independent validation. Do not repeat the evidence map
numerically.

## Required inferential transitions

- Landscape to discovery: the field map shows where causal methods entered the
  literature; the next question is what directed hypotheses they generated.
- Discovery to effects: directed hypotheses do not by themselves identify or
  quantify causal effects.
- Effects to validation: an identified effect remains design- and
  population-dependent until the same relation is examined independently.
- Validation to mechanisms: biological synthesis starts only after every
  relation has been assigned an evidence function.

## Appraisal strategy

Do not import OHAT or Kerr's generic high/medium/low labels as the sole appraisal
system. Use conditional design-family modules and report domain-level judgments.
At minimum, separate:

- identification assumptions and risk of bias;
- diagnostics, negative controls, and sensitivity analyses;
- multi-omics integration validity;
- discovery stability;
- validation independence;
- transportability across populations, tissues, systems, and species.

No summed score should combine these dimensions.

## Immediate dependency

Before Results can be written, causal extraction must convert the 101 eligible
reports into linked report, study/cohort, causal-analysis, and normalized-link
records. The final Level 2-4 synthesis count is an output of that extraction, not
the current PRISMA full-text count.
