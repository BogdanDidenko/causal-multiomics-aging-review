# Follow-up search for 10.1002/ccs3.70045

Search date: 2026-08-10

## Question

Did the authors subsequently perform the proposed work combining murine models
and multi-omics to determine how immune-senescence interactions drive myopia?

## Search coverage

The search used exact title, exact author combinations, and combinations of
`myopia`, `senescence`, `aging`, and `multi-omics` across PubMed/PMC, OpenAlex,
Crossref, bioRxiv, Research Square, and general scholarly web search. Author
identity was checked against affiliation and coauthor overlap because several
names are not unique.

## Findings

No direct follow-up study was found that combines multi-omics, myopia, and
cellular senescence by this author group.

Two related publications were identified:

1. **IL-18 receptor axis in allergic conjunctivitis: a multi-omics study**
   (10.3389/fimmu.2025.1704591) includes Wangming Su and Xiaoyong Yuan. It
   combines human GWAS analyses, mouse single-cell RNA sequencing, and in vivo
   validation. However, it concerns allergic conjunctivitis rather than myopia
   or aging/senescence. It was received on 2025-09-13, before the focal article
   was published on 2025-09-21, and is therefore best treated as parallel work,
   not a completed follow-up. The paper explicitly states that Mendelian
   randomization was not performed and that formal causal inference is outside
   its scope.
2. **NOX4 facilitates diabetic cataract progression through the NF-kappaB
   signaling pathway** (10.1016/j.exer.2025.110530) includes Wangming Su,
   Ping Xie, Pinsheng Qiu, and Yanling Li. It was published in July 2025,
   predates the focal article, concerns diabetic cataract, and does not provide
   the proposed myopia multi-omics follow-up.

## Screening implication

The follow-up search does not change the focal report's EC4 decision. Review
eligibility is assigned per report based on data analyzed in that report;
separate publications cannot retroactively create a second omics layer.
Neither related publication answers the review question of causal multi-omics
analysis of aging processes in myopia.
