# Review Question

## Primary Question

Which empirical studies integrate multiple molecular omics layers to
investigate a biological aging process or aging phenotype, and what causal
claim, directed hypothesis, or identification strategy do they support?

## PCC Framing

- **Population:** humans, animals, cells, tissues, organoids, or other
  biological systems used to investigate aging.
- **Concept:** at least two measured or analytically connected molecular omics
  layers; an aging process, phenotype, or intervention target; and a causal
  design or directed causal hypothesis.
- **Context:** primary articles and preprints indexed through 2026-07-27. No
  lower publication-date restriction is imposed at retrieval.

## Aging Boundary

Eligible aging concepts include biological aging or ageing, epigenetic age and
age acceleration, aging clocks, longevity, lifespan, healthspan, exceptional
longevity, geroscience, cellular senescence, rejuvenation, geroprotection, and
interventions intended to alter aging processes.

An older cohort, chronological age covariate, age-stratified analysis, or
age-related disease is not sufficient by itself. The report must analyze aging
as an outcome, mechanism, trajectory, exposure, mediator, or intervention
target.

## Causal Design Families

- genetic instrument, Mendelian randomization, or SMR/HEIDI;
- randomized intervention;
- non-randomized or quasi-experimental intervention;
- direct perturbation such as CRISPR, knockdown, knockout, or overexpression;
- temporal identification;
- formal mediation;
- graphical, structural-equation, Bayesian-network, or directed model with an
  explicit causal interpretation.

Author terminology is not an eligibility criterion. `Causal` wording alone is
insufficient, while an assessable design can qualify without using the phrases
`causal inference` or `causal discovery`.
